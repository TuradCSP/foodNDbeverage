1. Basics: Hygiene, Safety & HACCP

Hand Washing Steps (হাত ধোয়ার ধাপসমূহ)

  1. Wet hands: Use running water to wet your hands.
  2. Apply soap: Use approved hand-washing soap.
  3. Scrub: Vigorously scrub hands and arms for at least 20 seconds.
  4. Clean details: Clean under fingernails and between fingers.
  5. Rinse: Rinse thoroughly under running water.
  6. Dry and Turn off: Dry hands with a tissue and turn off the tap.

Hygiene & Cleanliness

  • Personal Hygiene: Personal grooming standards practiced in line with workplace health and safety requirements.
  • Workplace Hygiene: Preventing hygiene risks. The workplace area must be maintained in a clean and tidy order to meet standards. Facilities must be clean and maintained.
  • Food Hygiene: Food safety procedures.
  • Main reason for kitchen cleaning: To avoid cross-contamination and maintain hygiene.

Kitchen Safety (OHS) & Protocol

  • OHS (Occupational Health & Safety): Important in the hospitality industry to ensure workplace safety, reduce hazards and accidents, and ensure the safety of the receiver.
  • PPE (Personal Protective Equipment): Important for kitchen staff to avoid cross-contamination and maintain hygiene and cleanliness. Includes: Chef Hat (prevents hair falling), Face Mask (prevents respiratory droplets), Apron (protects from heat/spills), Gloves (ensures safe handling), Safety Shoes (non-slip).
  • Movement Compliance: Ensures food safety and safe food handling. You must walk cautiously; running to save time is not a good practice.
  • Health Conditions: Should be reported daily (or as required by the program), and necessary measures taken.
  • Who to talk to for safety concerns: Head Chef, Executive Chef, Kitchen Supervisor.
  • Defective Equipment: Must be separated and accordingly informed to the authority.
  • Delivery of Raw Materials: Check specification, order sheet, and regularly check the validity dates of materials.

HACCP Principles

Stands for: Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point

  1. Conduct a HAZARD analysis.
  2. Identify the critical control point.
  3. Establish critical line (limit).
  4. Monitor critical control point.
  5. Establish corrective actions.
  6. Verification.
  7. Keeping records.

Note: An SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) is essential for establishing a monitoring system in HACCP.

Important Terms & Documents

  • CBLM: Competency Based Learning Material.
  • SOP: Standard Operating Procedure / Protocol.
  • KOT/COT: Kitchen Order Ticket.
  • FIFO / LIFO: First in First out / Last in First out.
  • Mis-en-place: Preparation in advance.
  • Cross Contamination: Transferring bacteria from one place to another.
  • Work-documents: Email, Memo, Letter, SOP, KOT.

3. Stocks, Soups & Sauces

Stocks

A flavourful liquid agent which is made from meat/meat-bones and fish/fish-bones with aromatic vegetables, and bouquet garni (মসলার পোটলা) with spices.

  • Types: White Stock, Brown Stock, Fish Stock, Vegetable Stock.
  • Methods: Boiling (100°C) or Simmering/Semaring (80°C - 98°C).
  • Why does stock become cloudy? Quickly boiling or boiling too slowly.

Soups

A liquid dish typically savoury and made by boiling meat, fish or vegetables.

  • 3 Parts of Soup: 1. Stock (Base)
    2. Seasoning (Salt, Pepper)
    3. Garnishing (Croutons/Deep Fried Bread dice, Serials/Fries/Pans, Cheese/Parsley, Meat/Poultry, Cream, Seafoods/Pasta/Vegetables).
  • Types of Soup: 1. Cold soup (Gazpacho)
    2. International (Oxtail, French onion soup)
    3. Thin soup (Consomme or clear, and Broth)
    4. Thick soup (Cream, puree).

5 Mother Sauces

These serve as the starting point for many other sauces:

  • Bechamel: Also known as White Sauce.
  • Espagnole: Known as Brown Sauce.
  • Veloute: A savory sauce made from a roux and a light stock.
  • Concassee: Specifically listed as Tomato Sauce.
  • Hollandaise: An emulsion of egg yolk, melted butter, and an acid.

Thickening Agents

  • Roux: A combination of flour and butter cooked together. Types: White, brown, blond.
  • Corn flour, Rice flour, Potato starch, Egg yolk.
4. Salads, Meats, Fish & Bakery

Salads

A cold food which is made from raw and cooked vegetables or fruit, always served with dressing.

  • 4 Parts of Salad: Base (Lettuce leaf), Body (Cutting), Dressing (Consistency, colour, taste, and ingredients - mayo, sauce, potato), Garnishing (Olive oil, lemon, for decoration and taste).
  • Classification: 1. Simple salad (Continental, area-wise) 2. Mixed salad (Fusion/Flusion) 3. Composed salad.
  • Dressing: A seasoned liquid or semi-liquid that adds to the body of the salad to give flavour, taste, and color increase.
  • Russian Salad: Traditionally made of boiling potato, carrot, green peas, eggs, onions, & apples.

Fish, Shell-fish & Meat

  • Fish Classification: Round fish, Flat fish.
  • Shell-fish Classification: Crustaceans (Shrimp, Lobster, Prawns), Molluscs (Oyster, Mussels, Scallops).
  • Seafood Examples: Squid, octopus.
  • Meat Context: In general kitchen context, "meat" often refers to red meat like Mutton.
  • Egg Cooking Methods: Suitable cooking methods for eggs include Boiling and Poaching.

Bakery Style & Pastry

  • Stages of each dough & Baking: Scaling → Mixing → Fermentation (35° Temp, 92% humidity, 25-30 mins) → Make-up → Proofing (Same as fermentation conditions) → Baking → Finishing → Packaging.
  • Trivia: The muffin originated in France.
  • Puff Pastry: The primary leavening agent used in puff pastry is Butter.

Knives & Pans

  • Types of Knives: Chef knife (8-10 Inch long), Utility knife, Paring knife, Boning knife (separating meat from bone), Bread knife, Serrated knife, Meat cleaver, Carving knife.
  • Parts of Knives: Spine (top, blunt edge), Tip (pointed end), Handle (part you hold), Heels (back part of blade edge, near handle), Edge (sharp, cutting part).
  • Carrying Rule: In a busy kitchen, the knife should be carried downwards.
  • Cooking Pans: Sauce pan, Frying pan, Grilling pan, Roasting pan, Braising pan.

Cooking & Storage Temperatures

Chiller / Chillerant: 1°C to 4°C
Freezer: -18°C to -24°C
Boiling: 100°C
Cooking: 75°C
Re-heating: 75°C to 82°C
Simmering: 80°C to 98°C
Danger-zone: 5°C to 63°C
Yogurt Making: 37°C to 40°C
Room Temp: 37°C
Serving Temp: 63°C to 67°C
Dry-Storage: 10°C to 15°C
Deep-Fry: 160°C to 180°C
Vegetable Storage: 4°C to 10°C
Cold Storage: 10°C

Storage Times

Food and vegetables are stored at 4°C - 10°C. Fish Stock limit is 20 minutes.
